Trump Takes Personal Victory Lap, Chides Republicans for Losing

President credits himself with historic electoral success, faults Republican candidates, gravity, and the universe for party's defeat.

By Buck T. Bluster, Senior Presidential Perfection Correspondent

WASHINGTON, D.C., November 4, 2026 — President Donald Trump declared himself the overwhelming winner of Tuesday's midterm elections Wednesday morning, congratulating himself on an extraordinary personal victory while sharply criticizing Republican candidates for losing both houses of Congress.

Trump appeared before reporters at the White House with his tie hanging crookedly, hair in spectacular disarray, and a half-empty Diet Coke in his hand.

"I won bigly. Bigger than anybody. The Republicans? Terrible. They lost. Very embarrassing for them, frankly. I told them to win, but they didn't listen. Nobody listens anymore."

Over the next 47 minutes, the president identified numerous causes of the Republican disaster, none involving himself.

"The economy was tremendous. The war was beautiful. But the Democrats had the weather. Very unfair weather. And the moon. People don't talk about the moon enough."

White House aides hurriedly added lunar interference to a growing list of suspected electoral irregularities, which already included Chinese magnets, daylight saving time, Abraham Lincoln, and Tuesday.

"Tuesday's a terrible day for elections," Trump slurred, gripping the podium. "I've always said Wednesday. Beautiful Wednesday. Much better voting day."

Asked whether his policies contributed to the defeat, Trump dismissed the suggestion.

"My policies were perfect. Perfect policies. The voters were defective. Many people are saying this. Even the sun was against us. Came up in the east, very suspicious."

The White House subsequently announced a Presidential Commission on Universal Electoral Hostility, tasked with investigating the political affiliations of gravity, the solar system, and mathematics.

Trump concluded by announcing an executive order requiring the universe to apologize.

"Very badly run universe. Terrible leadership. I could've done a much better Big Bang. Bigger, hotter, tremendous bang. Everybody would still be talking about it."

The president then declared himself undefeated and demanded a recount of the planets.
