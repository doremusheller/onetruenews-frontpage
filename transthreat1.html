NATIONAL ACADEMY OF SCIENCES WARNS TRANSGENDER ATHLETE THREAT MAY BE GREATER THAN PREVIOUSLY FEARED

Leading mathematicians reveal alarming statistical similarities to hot soup, bees, and common classroom writing instruments.

By Dr. Mortimer P. Decimal, Senior Correspondent for Quantitative Emergencies

WASHINGTON, D.C. — The National Academy of Sciences confirmed that the transgender threat to American collegiate athletics may be considerably more dangerous than previously feared, following an emergency scientific assessment requested by the Trump administration, which provided the Academy with its conclusions in advance to expedite the research.

Dr. Edwin P. Worthington III, Director of Mathematical Integrity, delivered the findings with the grave composure of a man whose continued employment depended on making very small numbers sound very large.

According to December 2024 congressional testimony by NCAA President Charlie Baker, fewer than ten of approximately 510,000 NCAA athletes were known to be transgender, representing less than 0.002 percent.

"That is an exceptionally small number," Worthington acknowledged. "Indeed, so small that many Americans may find themselves insufficiently frightened. This would be a mistake."

To demonstrate the magnitude of the threat, Worthington compared the findings to other dangers facing ordinary Americans.

"The National Safety Council estimates the lifetime odds of death from contact with hot surfaces or substances at approximately one in 45,000. Consider soup."

Worthington paused.

"Soup is frequently served at temperatures capable of causing injury. It travels in unsecured vessels, often through crowded dining facilities, and is consumed inches from the human face. Yet Americans approach soup with astonishing complacency."

He adjusted his glasses.

"We are not suggesting all soup is dangerous. Merely that Americans have grown dangerously comfortable with soup."

The findings became more disturbing when Worthington addressed bees.

Americans face lifetime odds of approximately one in 42,626 of dying from hornet, wasp, or bee stings.

"These insects operate in organized colonies, communicate through sophisticated signaling systems, and carry concealed biological weapons. Some colonies contain 50,000 individuals. We do not know how many are transgender NCAA athletes. Almost certainly none. But almost certainly is not certainly."

Finally, Worthington addressed pencils.

The National Safety Council estimates lifetime odds of death involving sharp objects at approximately one in 20,599.

"Pencils are deliberately sharpened, distributed to millions of schoolchildren, and carried openly without registration. Many possess erasers, permitting the destruction of written evidence."

Asked whether comparing lifetime mortality risks to the prevalence of transgender college athletes was mathematically legitimate, Worthington acknowledged that the measurements involved entirely different populations, denominators, and definitions.

"Nevertheless," he explained, "both contain numbers. And numbers are the foundation of mathematics."

The White House welcomed the findings, announcing that the Trump administration was investigating reports of transgender bees and soup workers with equal severity.

A spokesperson expressed particular concern about transgender bees employed in soup production, although no such cases had been documented.

Worthington declined further comment, explaining that the Academy had been ordered to investigate the statistical significance of zero.

"Under ordinary mathematical principles, multiplying zero by any other number still produces zero," he said.

He paused.

"Of course, those principles are currently under review."
