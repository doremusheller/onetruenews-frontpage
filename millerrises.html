MILLER RISES AS WHITE HOUSE INSTALLS PRESIDENTIAL ACCESS INTEGRITY SYSTEM

New protocol routes visitors, briefings, and unscreened reality through Miller's office; aides say historical comparisons are “liberal pattern-matching.”

By Vera R. Gate, Senior White House Access Correspondent

WASHINGTON, D.C., November 7, 2026 — Stephen Miller emerged as the administration's central authority on presidential access Saturday, announcing a new White House protocol requiring all visitors, briefings, and unsupervised thoughts about current events to pass through his office.

The Presidential Access Integrity System will prohibit staff from dropping in to see President Donald Trump without an approved appointment, a written purpose, and a preliminary review by Miller's team. Incoming memos will be screened for relevance, tone, and whether they might cause the president to ask a follow-up question.

“This is not a gate,” Miller told reporters beside a waist-high velvet rope. “It is a process that ensures the president receives the people and information most worthy of his time. Which, at this moment, is me.”

Officials said Trump remains free to speak with anyone he chooses, provided Miller first confirms the person is available, the subject is appropriate, and the conversation will not introduce unnecessary facts.

The system drew comparisons to Martin Bormann, Hitler’s private secretary and head of the Nazi Party Chancellery. The German History in Documents and Images project describes Bormann as controlling access to Hitler and routing party business through himself. At Nuremberg, testimony recorded that officials often used Bormann’s channel because he had daily access to Hitler.

Miller dismissed the parallel as “another example of liberals seeing Nazis everywhere,” adding that he had never heard of Bormann and would not recognize a comparison even if one were submitted in the proper format.

“They make these comparisons because they are obsessed with history,” Miller said. “We are focused on the future, which is why no one may discuss it with the president without my permission.”

By late afternoon, the president had reportedly submitted a request to speak with his senior adviser. Miller’s office said it would respond after reviewing the president’s stated purpose.
